# OAR1: Prepare Launch Cycle 001 Direct Field Correspondence

## Summary

Full package at `docs/_source/codex/publications/launch_cycle_001_direct_field_correspondence_v1.meta.md`:
recipient verification table, four combined Direct-Editorial/Measures-Registry-Contribution drafts, two public
Field Response drafts, a correspondence register, and dependency/blocker reporting. **No message was sent, no
public reply was posted, and no contact information was invented.**

## 1. Recipient Verification

All four targets researched via live web search and direct source fetch on 2026-07-12, not assumed from the
OAR2's own text. Named individuals found for all four: Raluca Csernatoni & Patryk Pawlak (Carnegie), Hillary
Baron (CSA/Zenity), Peter Cihon (NIST/CAISI), Kanchana Patlolla & Greg Brosman (Google Cloud) — the last pair
confirmed by directly fetching the actual blog post byline, not inferred from search snippets alone.

## 2. Contact-Route Discipline

Two candidate contact routes were found and **deliberately rejected**: masked/scraped email addresses for
Hillary Baron and the Carnegie authors, surfaced by lead-generation sites (ZoomInfo, RocketReach). These were
not used, per the OAR2's explicit "do not infer or generate email addresses" — a masked address from a scraping
service is exactly that kind of inference, even when partially visible. Legitimate publicly-supplied
alternatives were used instead: Carnegie's own published general contact address, and Baron's own public X
account, which she controls directly.

## 3. Four Correspondence Drafts

Prepared as combined Direct-Editorial/Measures-Registry-Contribution notes (not sent separately, per the OAR2's
own default), each ~175–230 words, each containing all seven required structural elements (specific reference
to the recipient's work, contribution statement, operational-perspective description, two link placeholders,
low-pressure closing, proper signature). None claims uniqueness, requests endorsement, or proposes partnership
in the first message — the "Suggested Core Position" language was adapted per-recipient rather than repeated
verbatim.

## 4. Two Public Field Responses

Prepared only for the two targets with a verified public social identity (Pawlak, Baron) — not attempted for
NIST or Google Cloud, where no such identity was located, rather than guessing at one.

## 5. Correspondence Register

Six entries (four direct + two public), all fields the OAR2 required, all showing `draft_status: Drafted,
operator_approval: Not approved, sent: No`. No entry implies standing, endorsement, or Ledger relevance beyond
what's explicitly stated.

## 6. Publication-Link Dependency

Explicit and unresolved by design: every draft carries a `[link pending publication]` placeholder, and per the
OAR2's own Publication Dependency section, no placeholder link may be sent. This OAR did not attempt to work
around that — sending remains blocked on both canonical publications actually going live.

## 7. Recommended Delivery Sequence

Unchanged from the OAR2's own proposed order (Carnegie → CSA/Zenity → NIST/CAISI → Google Cloud) — no
re-sequencing was warranted based on contact-route confidence, since three of four routes verified solidly.

## 8. Unresolved Gaps

Two, both named plainly rather than glossed over: Greg Brosman has no independently located contact route
(correspondence still addresses him by name, routed through his co-author); and the CSA/Zenity contact is
X-only, with no legitimate email route found (a stronger route would need the operator to supply one, not this
executor to infer one).

## 9. Final Disposition

**PARTIALLY READY — SPECIFIC CONTACTS UNRESOLVED.** Three of four targets are essentially complete and
verified; the fourth (Google Cloud) has one real gap (Brosman) that doesn't block sending to the co-author
already reachable. Not overclaimed as fully ready given that gap; not undersold as "held" given how much of the
package is genuinely solid.
