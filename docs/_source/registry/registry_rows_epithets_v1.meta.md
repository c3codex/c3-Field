# Registry Rows Epithets

## Purpose

Define the planted epithet-row family for the rewritten registry source stack.

This doc preserves the epithet family as the 9 role units.

An epithet row exists to stabilize role through planted registry seating without collapsing into:

- spine rows
- chamber-directory rows
- gate rows
- ME rows
- chamberplate
- access readability

This doc defines the epithet-family content surface.

It does not define:

- row identity shape in general
- geometry rules in general
- structural validity conditions in general
- chamber-directory content
- chamberplate row content
- gate-family content
- ME-family content

## Scope

For the current rewritten pass, the seated epithet rows are:

- Epithet I
- Epithet II
- Epithet III
- Epithet IV
- Epithet V
- Epithet VI
- Epithet VII
- Epithet VIII
- Epithet IX

This doc is family-specific.
It is not the whole registry stack.

## Epithet-Family Rule

Epithet rows are:

- planted Measures units
- role-bearing rows
- stabilized role seats
- sequence-bearing within the epithet family

Epithets remain the 9 role units.

They do not collapse into:

- gates, which are thresholded progression units
- spine rows, which are system-known and non-planted
- chamber directories, which communicate chamber-local information
- MEs, which are the 13 function units

## Oracle Alignment Note

The original oracle-card alignment remains specifically tied to:

- obsidian
- crystal
- marble

That original oracle alignment is preserved in this doc.

Lapis Lazuli is the 4th native material in the wider system, but it is not used to retroactively rewrite the original oracle-card material alignment.

## Material Seating Rule

Role family and material family remain distinct.

For epithet rows, this means:

- every epithet row must eventually resolve to one of the original oracle materials:
  - obsidian
  - crystal
  - marble
- epithet rows may not be flattened into one single material family
- epithet rows may not be rewritten into lapis simply because lapis now has a rightful system place elsewhere

In other words:

Epithets = the 9 role units  
Oracle alignment = obsidian / crystal / marble  
Lapis = wider native material, not a retrofit override

## Access Note

Epithet seating remains real even where exhibition readability includes chamber directories or multiple access paths.

That means:

- chamber-directory presence does not replace epithet seating
- access readability does not replace role seating
- visibility of oracle-related surfaces does not by itself prove conversion standing

## Conversion Note

Conversion remains completion standing.

Epithet rows may participate in the relational path toward conversion.
They do not themselves equal conversion standing.

A visible, readable, or draw-able oracle/epithet surface is not the same thing as completed conversion.

## Circuit Note

c1, c2, and c3 remain circuit only.

For the current rewritten epithet-family pass, the clean working read is:

- epithet rows are seated primarily as c2-facing role stabilization rows

That keeps epithets distinct from:

- c1 intake
- c2 gates as thresholded progression units
- c3 ME rows as function-bearing units

Later cross-family relation may expose stronger c3 adjacency without changing the epithet family itself.

## Epithet-Row Rules

### Role-Seat Rule

Each epithet row remains a planted role seat.

It may stabilize function and relation.
It does not become:

- a gate
- a spine row
- a chamber directory
- an ME
- a chamberplate

### Material-Alignment Rule

Each epithet row must resolve materially within the original oracle alignment:

- obsidian
- crystal
- marble

No epithet row may be flattened into a one-material family by default.
No epithet row may be assigned lapis as a rewrite of the original oracle alignment.

### Family-Order Rule

The epithet family remains a 9-row family.

Its order is:

1. Epithet I
2. Epithet II
3. Epithet III
4. Epithet IV
5. Epithet V

- the epithet family remains 9 rows
