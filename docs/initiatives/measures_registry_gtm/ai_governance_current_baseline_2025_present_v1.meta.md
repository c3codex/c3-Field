# AI Governance Current Baseline — 2025 to Present

## Purpose
Establish the current-era comparison layer against the pre-2025 historical baseline. This baseline tracks where consequential AI is scaling, where governance investment is being added, what organizations and regulators say that governance is intended to control, and what consequential cracks or boundary failures have already surfaced.

This artifact does not establish Registry standing, prove the Measures Registry thesis, authorize publication, or claim that specialized AI governance is ineffective.

## Comparative Method
For each cohort preserve:
`AI expansion → governance response/investment → buyer/owner → control intent → documented crack/failure → consequence → remediation/additional control → failure classification → system/environment relevance → confidence`.

Primary classification values:
- `AI_or_model_defect`
- `specialized_governance_failure`
- `computational_system_environment_failure`
- `mixed`
- `insufficient_evidence`

Seven integrity conditions remain unchanged:
`Ownership → Authority → Position → Identity → Runtime → Evidence → Response`.

Governance Discontinuity remains a held research candidate.

## Cohort 001 — Healthcare
### Expansion / Governance Investment
- Inova Health selected Signal 1's AI management platform in December 2025 for AI deployment and oversight.
- Mount Sinai Health System partnered with Signal 1 in June 2026 to strengthen AI governance and accelerate value realization.
- NewYork-Presbyterian adopted Signal 1's AI management system in August 2026 to support innovation and patient safety.
- Hartford HealthCare partnered with Vitea in May 2026 for AI oversight across an integrated health system.
- Mercyhealth selected Vitea in August 2026 for end-to-end AI visibility, policy enforcement, and accountability as AI expanded across clinical and operational workflows.

### Governance Signal
Healthcare has moved from informal AI review toward named governance/management platforms, system-wide visibility, centralized intake/oversight, policy enforcement, and portfolio management.

### Current Crack / Open Integrity Question
The strongest current question is not yet a post-platform failure case; it is whether these investments successfully govern local AI portfolios while institutional/runtime variability, vendor evidence, ownership, workflow authority, and cross-system lifecycle conditions continue to require separate reconstruction.

### Standing
`strong_current_governance_investment_baseline; longitudinal_failure_evidence_pending`

## Cohort 002 — Financial Services / Banking
### Expansion / Governance Formation
- Midsize and large banks are expanding from conventional analytics into generative and agentic AI.
- Valley Bank publicly described AI as changing the traditional build-versus-buy calculus for a $64B-asset institution, with tiered employee access used to control cost and risk.
- UK banks including NatWest, Lloyds, and Starling were preparing agentic-AI consumer trials for 2026 under FCA engagement.
- MetaComp launched a Know Your Agent governance framework in April 2026 centered on agent identity, authorization, monitoring, and accountability in regulated financial workflows.

### Governance Signal
Federal banking regulators revised model-risk guidance in April 2026 and explicitly stated that generative and agentic AI fall outside the traditional model-risk-management framework, while simultaneously indicating that broader governance and risk-management practices must control those systems. Regulators are increasingly asking banks about high-risk AI uses, vendor/subcontractor oversight, system controls, contingency plans, and kill-switch mechanisms.

### Current Crack / Boundary Signal
The present crack is category and authority discontinuity: mature model-risk-management structures do not cleanly cover generative/agentic systems, while AI capability is moving into autonomous/customer-facing contexts. Governance responsibility is therefore being reconstructed through other institutional controls rather than inherited from the traditional model-governance regime.

### Classification
`governance_discontinuity_candidate; consequence_not_yet_established_for_named_US_bank_case`

### Standing
`strong_expansion_and_regulatory_boundary_signal; named_consequential_failure_pending`

## Cohort 003 — Insurance
### Expansion
NAIC surveys show very high AI/ML adoption or planned adoption across insurance lines; among responding health insurers, 92% reported using, planning to use, or exploring AI/ML. AI affects underwriting, pricing, claims, marketing, fraud, and customer interaction.

### Governance Investment / Regulatory Response
- The NAIC Model Bulletin established governance expectations beginning in 2023 and continued expanding through state adoption and implementation.
- In 2025–2026 NAIC developed an AI Systems Evaluation Tool for market-conduct, financial-analysis, and financial-exam use.
- As of March 2026 the tool was being piloted by 12 states.
- NAIC continues separate work on third-party data/model accountability and market-conduct examination guidance.

### Current Crack / Boundary Signal
The dominant current integrity issue is third-party and decision-boundary accountability: insurers may rely on vendors to develop, deploy, and manage systems that affect underwriting, claims, and utilization decisions, while regulators still need a consistent mechanism to determine what system is being used, how it is governed, what data it relies on, and how responsibility is carried across vendor-insurer boundaries.

### Consequential Case Link
Ongoing Medicare Advantage litigation around predictive coverage tools remains a high-value longitudinal case but allegations and adjudicated findings must remain separated. Current regulator activity independently confirms that third-party models, individualized decision authority, and post-deployment testing are live governance concerns.

### Standing
`strong_marketwide_governance_formation; third_party_boundary_reconstruction_supported; merits_of_named_litigation_held`

## Cohort 004 — Government / Public Sector
### Expansion
GAO reported in July 2025 that across 11 selected federal agencies, reported AI use cases nearly doubled from 571 in 2023 to 1,110 in 2024, while generative-AI use cases rose from 32 to 282. Agencies reported rapid evolution, policy compliance, budget, technical capability, and policy-maintenance challenges.

### Governance / Procurement Response
GAO's 2026 acquisition review found agencies using a wide variety of acquisition approaches for AI, including computer vision and deep-learning systems. Agencies were not systematically collecting acquisition lessons learned; officials cited difficulty accessing technical expertise and understanding AI-related costs. GAO noted lost opportunities to reuse knowledge such as contract terms for data rights and testing.

### Current Crack
`local_acquisition_governance_without_reliable_cross_acquisition_learning`

Each acquisition can be reviewed and governed locally while evidence, contract learning, technical understanding, testing requirements, and governance lessons fail to persist across the institution.

### Classification
`governance_discontinuity_candidate_with_operational_cost`

### Consequence
Documented consequence currently consists of duplicated acquisition effort, limited technical/economic visibility, and lost opportunities for institutional learning; not yet a single public catastrophic failure attributable to this condition.

### Standing
`strong_current_discontinuity_evidence`

### Sources
- https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-25-107653.pdf
- https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-26-107859.pdf

## Cohort 005 — Industrial / Critical Infrastructure / Physical Autonomy
### Current Failure Case — Ford BlueCruise fatal crashes
NTSB's March 31, 2026 findings on two fatal 2024 crashes involving Ford's BlueCruise partial-automation system identified driver overreliance, ineffective driver monitoring, gaps in system implementation, inaccessible crash data, and lack of federal standards/oversight as contributing system conditions.

### Structural Significance
This is a strong modern comparison with the 2018 Uber ATG case. The technology and organizational context changed, but several environmental conditions recur:
- ambiguous/fragile human fallback role;
- automation overreliance;
- inadequate monitoring of the human-system relationship;
- incomplete evidence capture for investigation;
- insufficient external oversight/standardization.

### Classification
`mixed_with_strong_system_environment_component`

### Integrity Relevance
Ownership, Authority, Position, Runtime, Evidence, Response.

### Standing
`high_confidence_current_recurrence_case`

## Cohort 006 — Education
### Higher Education Expansion
EDUCAUSE reported in 2025 that:
- 63% of QuickPoll respondents said procurement processes already account for AI or will;
- 45% cited inability to keep up with rapid AI-product change as a top challenge;
- 40% cited insufficient institutional AI governance;
- 86% said AI products were used for teaching and learning while only 58% included teaching-and-learning professionals in AI procurement;
- only 36% of institutions with AI-related procurement processes said products were re-reviewed after initial purchase to account for new functions or developments.

Separate EDUCAUSE research found 50% of institutions increasing AI access, 37% funding licenses, 38% adapting models internally, while only 9% said existing cybersecurity/privacy policies sufficiently addressed AI risks.

### Current Crack
`distributed_use_outpacing_governance_and_re_review`

The most important current signal is that AI products spread through academic, administrative, and operational functions while governance, procurement participation, policy, and ongoing product review remain uneven.

### K-12 / Student-System Expansion
Federal Student Aid reported in 2025–2026 that AI-enabled fraud rings and bots were exploiting student-aid systems, leading to nationwide identity-verification and real-time fraud-detection controls. The Department reported more than $1B in prevented federal-aid fraud in 2025 and later embedded real-time risk-based identity screening into FAFSA.

### Structural Significance
Education now contains both directions of consequential computation:
1. institutions deploying AI into advising, procurement, teaching, administration, and student-support functions; and
2. external AI-enabled actors exploiting institutional identity and benefits systems.

Both stress identity, authority, evidence, review, and cross-office response paths.

### Standing
`strong_governance_gap_and_identity_boundary_signal; named_institutional_AI_failure_case_pending`

## Cross-Industry Current-Era Pattern
The 2025-present record shows three simultaneous developments:

1. **AI capability is becoming operationally distributed** across clinical, financial, insurance, public, physical, and educational systems.
2. **Governance investment is increasing** through platforms, committees, regulator tools, revised procurement, model/agent controls, and external oversight.
3. **Several historical integrity conditions are still visible**: evidence/validation, authority, role/position, third-party responsibility, runtime monitoring, ongoing review, and institutional learning across boundaries.

This does not yet establish that current AI governance is failing. It establishes that the present market is actively paying to reconstruct or strengthen many of the same governance conditions observed before the current spending boom.

## Highest-Value Longitudinal Questions
1. Do named healthcare systems that purchased AI-governance platforms later experience failures outside the scope those products govern?
2. Does agentic banking force new identity/authorization/return controls because existing model-risk regimes do not carry them?
3. Can insurers produce coherent evidence and accountability across third-party model/vendor boundaries during examination and claims disputes?
4. Do federal agencies improve acquisition learning, or does governance continue to be reconstructed contract by contract?
5. Do BlueCruise-style role, evidence, and oversight failures recur in newer physical/agentic systems despite added safety governance?
6. Do universities establish ongoing review and cross-stakeholder authority fast enough to match AI-product mutation and distributed use?

## Holds

- Do not classify market expansion as a crack without documented governance consequence.
- Do not convert litigation allegations into adjudicated facts.
- Do not equate specialized AI governance with computational systems governance.
