# AI Governance Named Watchlist — 2025 to Present

## Purpose
Move below sector-level market evidence into named longitudinal targets. Preserve organizations where AI adoption, governance investment, governance formation, or consequential failure creates a useful baseline for future comparison.

For each organization preserve:
`organization → sector → AI scale/use → governance investment/control → named vendor/advisor → disclosed/estimated spend → stated purpose → current crack/tension → consequence → future watch condition → source quality → standing`.

Do not infer that a current buyer will fail. Governance purchases are treated as control baselines, not evidence of failure.

## Cohort 001 — Healthcare

### Mount Sinai Health System
- AI expansion: imaging, generative AI, emerging agent-based systems across seven hospital campuses plus academic/research settings.
- Governance investment: evaluating Signal 1 AIMS for centralized AI visibility, intake/approval, monitoring/reporting, performance optimization, and ROI/impact tracking.
- Named executive: Robbie Freeman, Chief Digital Transformation Officer.
- Spend: undisclosed.
- Baseline promise: centralized oversight and lifecycle governance for a diverse AI portfolio.
- Future watch: whether cross-departmental AI remains visible; whether local workflow, evidence, authority, or change conditions still require separate reconstruction.
- Standing: `high_value_longitudinal_control`.

### Mercyhealth
- AI expansion: clinical workflows, patient outcomes, and operational efficiency across regional hospitals/care sites.
- Governance investment: Vitea for enterprise AI visibility, policy enforcement, accountability, monitoring, and control of shadow/embedded AI.
- Named executive: Ali Olia, CIO.
- Spend: undisclosed.
- Baseline promise: all AI visible, controlled, policy-compliant, and accountable.
- Future watch: incident response, policy exceptions, vendor changes, shadow-AI recurrence, local clinical authority and evidence passage.
- Standing: `high_value_longitudinal_control`.

### Hartford HealthCare
- Governance investment: Vitea platform to strengthen AI oversight across a large integrated system.
- Spend: undisclosed.
- Future watch: expansion into clinical care, operations, and patient engagement; whether governance centralization resolves cross-workflow conditions or requires local compensatory structures.
- Standing: `longitudinal_control`.

## Cohort 002 — Financial Services / Banking

### JPMorgan Chase
- AI scale: proprietary generative AI platform reportedly used by more than 200,000 employees; large enterprise AI program.
- Investment signal: public reporting cites approximately $2B in AI investment and a roughly $20B annual technology budget; treat exact AI figure as reported, not audited here.
- Governance context: mature bank risk, compliance, model-risk, cybersecurity, and third-party-control environment.
- Future watch: agentic expansion, cross-system permissions, tool execution, third-party dependencies, and whether existing model-risk governance travels into generative/agentic systems.
- Standing: `high_scale_watch_target`.

### Deutsche Bank
- AI expansion: agentic TPRM AI implemented in Global Procurement & Vendor Management in December 2025.
- Consequential role: evaluates documentary vendor evidence against control frameworks in third-party onboarding/review.
- Governance significance: agentic AI is being inserted into a governance process itself.
- Future watch: who retains evidentiary judgment, exception authority, escalation, audit trail, and responsibility when the agent performs review work.
- Standing: `high_value_environment_test`.

### Fiserv / six co-developing financial institutions
- Expansion: agentOS launched for agents across core banking, payments, issuer processing, servicing, AML, deposit operations, risk, and regulatory reporting.
- Governance claim: identity-bound execution, policy enforcement, observability, traceability, human oversight, curated third-party agent marketplace.
- Named institutional partners: six financial institutions not fully identified in the public launch.
- Future watch: third-party agent authority, marketplace interoperability, shared identity/audit controls, and how governance persists across bank/Fiserv/agent-provider boundaries.
- Standing: `high_value_agentic_control_baseline`.

### Regulatory boundary signal
- 2026 U.S. interagency model-risk guidance explicitly excludes generative and agentic AI from scope while directing banks to apply broader governance/risk practices.
- APRA reported in April 2026 that governance, risk management, assurance, and operational resilience practices at selected banks/insurers were not keeping pace with AI adoption.
- Interpretation: mature financial governance exists, but its transfer into new AI forms remains an active institutional problem.

## Cohort 003 — Insurance

### UnitedHealth Group
- AI governance baseline: 2026 proxy describes board committee oversight, Chief Legal Officer/Legal & Compliance ownership, Responsible AI Program Office, AI solution inventory, internal AI Review Board with authority to approve or remove models, and internal/external advisory bodies.
- Important timing: UHG states AI governance ownership was strengthened in 2024.
- Consequential domains: clinical, coverage, operational processes, member/provider interactions.
- Spend: not isolated publicly in reviewed evidence.
- Longitudinal value: unusually strong documented governance baseline against which litigation, regulatory findings, operational outcomes, and model-use disputes can be compared.
- Standing: `highest_value_insurance_longitudinal_case`.

### Allianz
- Expansion: global 2026 partnership with Anthropic for workforce use, agentic operations automation, and regulatory compliance.
- Governance promise: responsible/safe AI with transparency and regulatory alignment.
- Spend: undisclosed.
- Future watch: agent authority, customer-impacting operations, compliance evidence, and third-party model dependency across a multinational insurance environment.
- Standing: `high_value_longitudinal_control`.

### Sector governance formation
- NAIC AI Systems Evaluation Tool is being piloted by 12 states in 2026 to gather insurer AI inventories, governance/risk practices, high-risk-model information, and data inputs.
- Texas and other states have issued 2025-2026 insurer AI governance expectations extending responsibility to third-party systems.
- Interpretation: regulators themselves are constructing shared visibility/evidence mechanisms because insurer-local governance does not automatically provide regulator-ready standing across boundaries.
- Standing: `governance_discontinuity_candidate_sector_level`.

## Cohort 004 — Government / Public Sector

### DOD / DHS / GSA / VA
- GAO 2026 reviewed 13 AI acquisitions across selected federal agencies.
- Expansion: AI acquired through contracts, licenses, services, OTAs, and vendor-introduced capabilities.
- Spend signal: DOD awarded four July 2025 OTAs with $200M ceilings each to major AI companies for advanced AI adoption.
- Governance crack: agencies were not systematically collecting lessons learned; officials said they often had to figure out AI acquisition independently.
- Specific recurring gaps: data rights, testing terms, vendor accountability, access to technical expertise, evolving contract requirements, and inability in some procurements to include desired post-award testing.
- Consequence: lost ability to reuse best practices and avoid repeated procurement mistakes; GAO issued recommendations to all four agencies.
- Classification: `strong_governance_discontinuity_candidate`.

### Project Maven lineage
- GAO reports early Maven contracts lacked AI-related requirements sufficient to hold vendors accountable; later contracts added increasingly specific AI requirements.
- Structural signal: governance requirements were learned and reconstructed through repeated acquisition rather than carried from the beginning.
- Standing: `strong_reconstruction_dependency_candidate`.

### GSA government-wide AI acquisition
- By 2025-2026, GSA created common acquisition channels for OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and other models and an AI evaluation suite.
- Future watch: whether centralized procurement successfully carries testing, ownership, security, audit, and change conditions into downstream agency environments.
- Standing: `important_control_case`.

## Cohort 005 — Industrial / Critical Infrastructure / Physical Autonomy

### Ford BlueCruise / partial driving automation
- Current-era crack: NTSB 2026 findings concerning two fatal 2024 crashes identify overreliance, driver-monitoring limitations, implementation gaps, crash-evidence accessibility issues, and inadequate standards/oversight.
- Historical comparison: shares several structural conditions with the 2018 Uber ATG baseline despite different company, architecture, and governance context.
- Classification: `current_recurrence_candidate`.
- Future watch: remediation, driver-monitoring changes, evidence accessibility, operational-design-domain constraints, and regulator response.

### Tech Mahindra / Mahindra Group / ServiceNow
- Expansion: 2026 multi-year partnership to move enterprise AI beyond pilots; Mahindra Group and Tech Mahindra operate as Client Zero environments.
- Governance investment: ServiceNow AI Platform, AI Control Tower, new AI/Innovation CoE, cross-industry deployment including manufacturing.
- Scale signal: ServiceNow already integrated across Tech Mahindra global IT operations handling over 100,000 cases monthly across 90 countries.
- Future watch: whether governance/control-tower architecture can preserve authority, role, evidence, and change conditions as agents expand across enterprise and industrial workflows.
- Standing: `high_value_control_case`.

### Sector observation
- Industrial/critical infrastructure remains the thinnest public-spend cohort for dedicated AI-governance purchases, but the strongest physical-consequence cohort for testing whether system/environment conditions remain coherent under automation.
- Do not substitute generic AI investment announcements for governance evidence.

## Cohort 006 — Education

- Procurement: 2025 sole-source award notice estimated up to $25M over five years for ChatGPT across university educational, administrative, and research tasks.
- Scope: broad institution-wide environment rather than a narrow pilot.
- Governance significance: one of the clearest disclosed higher-education AI spending baselines.
- Future watch: central vs departmental authority, data handling, academic-integrity policy, research use, accessibility, changing capabilities, and continuing review.

### California State University
- Current-era expansion: public reporting describes a $17M systemwide ChatGPT investment across campuses.
- Current crack: 2026 reporting describes continuing distrust, inconsistent academic-integrity enforcement, false AI-use accusations, and extreme local monitoring practices.
- Classification: `environment_governance_tension_supported`; do not infer platform purchase caused the integrity conflict.
- Structural value: centrally scaled capability coexists with locally divergent authority, evidence standards, and review practices.
- Standing: `high_value_longitudinal_case`.

### University of Maine System
- Expansion: first systemwide enterprise AI platform contract, ChatGPT Edu, available to faculty, staff, and matriculated students from July 2026.
- Contract: two-year agreement; value not disclosed in reviewed source.
- Governance promise: responsible integration plus workforce readiness and institutional efficiency.
- Future watch: systemwide policy coherence vs campus/local academic autonomy and changing use cases.
- Standing: `longitudinal_control`.

### Stanford University
- Expansion: ChatGPT Edu, Gemini Enterprise, and Claude for Education campus pilot through August 2027, initiated partly because users were purchasing tools individually.
- Governance significance: central procurement explicitly attempts to bring previously distributed/individual AI use under better data protection and institutional terms.

- Future watch: whether central tooling reduces shadow use while academic authority/evidence remains fragmented.
- Standing: `high_value_governance_formation_case`.

### University of Kent
- Procurement: enterprise generative-AI platform estimated at £2M excluding VAT during market engagement; contract awarded in 2026.
- Stated purpose: secure, institutionally governed environment spanning teaching, learning, research, accessibility, and operational efficiency.

- Standing: `high_value_explicit_environment_control_case`.

## Cross-Industry Signals Below the Topsoil

### Signal 1 — Governance spend is moving from policy to infrastructure
Named buyers are purchasing or building platforms that promise visibility, identity, policy enforcement, evidence, monitoring, lifecycle control, and auditability. This means the market is increasingly paying for operational governance rather than policy documents alone.

### Signal 2 — Agentic expansion is forcing old governance boundaries open
Banking, government, healthcare, insurance, and enterprise systems are moving from predictive/generative tools into agents that execute, route, review, transact, or act through other systems. Existing model-risk and policy frameworks often do not automatically cover these execution conditions.

### Signal 3 — Third-party boundaries recur everywhere
Healthcare vendors, bank technology providers, insurer models, government contractors, university AI vendors, and enterprise agent marketplaces all recreate the same question: what governance conditions persist when consequential work crosses organizational/vendor boundaries?

### Signal 4 — Central governance does not necessarily eliminate local reconstruction
Education is the clearest current example: centralized AI procurement can coexist with highly local academic-integrity, assessment, evidence, and authority practices. Healthcare and government show analogous patterns in clinical and acquisition environments.

### Signal 5 — The strongest present evidence is not yet widespread post-purchase failure
Most 2025-2026 named governance purchases are too recent for meaningful longitudinal failure evidence. Their value is as prospective control baselines. The research engine should not manufacture failure; it should preserve exactly what was promised and watch what happens next.
