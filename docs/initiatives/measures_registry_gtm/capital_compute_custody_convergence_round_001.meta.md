## Research question

Are major capital-rich institutions merely investing in AI compute, or are they simultaneously manufacturing private computational-system coherence around that compute?

## Guardrail

Do not infer secret motive, concealment, authorization, or wrongdoing from private infrastructure or non-public data. Treat secrecy/privacy as a strategic custody incentive only where supported by institutional disclosures about confidential, proprietary, customer, or non-public data.

## Findings

### Equifax — strongest convergence control

Observed:
- Since 2018, Equifax says it invested approximately $3B in Equifax Cloud, its cloud-native security and technology infrastructure.
- More than 90% of revenue ran in Equifax Cloud by year-end 2025; 46 data centers had been decommissioned since the transformation began.
- Equifax integrated AI governance into its business-as-usual ERM framework.
- 100% of new models and scores in 2025 used EFX.AI.
- Equifax explicitly describes its scale proprietary data as the foundation of its “AI data moat” and a major competitive advantage, including unique, non-public data assets.
- Security roadmap: passwordless workforce, machine/non-human identity verification, protocols for first-generation AI agents, and expansion of Policy-as-Code to test thousands of agents for safety, drift, and privacy.

MR read:
This is the strongest observed example of infrastructure consolidation + proprietary-data custody + machine identity + policy/runtime governance converging in one institutional environment. The institution is not just governing models; it is engineering the substrate in which models and agents operate.

### JPMorganChase — controlled shared environment at enormous scale

Observed:
- 2026 technology budget approximately $19.8B.
- Technology estate includes more than 6,000 applications and nearly an exabyte of data, using multi-cloud infrastructure.
- Firm states that data scale is a strategic asset.
- LLM Suite rolled to more than 200,000 employees in a controlled environment that protects customer and company data and provides a shared capability layer for enterprise workflows.
- CDAO sets firmwide strategy, governance, risk controls, data management/quality, model-risk policies, review frameworks, and tooling.
- More than 500 AI use cases were reported in production in 2025.

MR read:
This resembles deliberate environmental coherence: centralized governance/tooling + federated execution + protected data + approved shared platform + production monitoring. It is an upper-bound control for what a capital-rich institution can manufacture internally.

### Bank of America — internal system control + external infrastructure capital

Observed internal environment:
- More than $100B invested in technology over the prior decade; $13B in technology spending in 2025, including more than $4B in new technology initiatives.
- Internal compliance and operational-risk functions monitor control effectiveness, third-party dependencies, quality assurance, issue identification, remediation, escalation, and resilience.
- 2025 10-K explicitly warns that third-party AI services and autonomous agents may interact with Bank systems or employees, act without authorization, make execution errors, behave unpredictably, or create unintended consequences.
- Bank says it has internal AI processes and governance frameworks integrated with broader compliance and operational-risk controls.

Observed capital role:
- August 2026: announced a $250B, 18-month Critical Infrastructure Finance Initiative covering digital infrastructure including data centers, computing hardware/chips/equipment, telecommunications and semiconductors, plus energy/power infrastructure.

MR read:
This is the clearest capital-to-environment convergence case: the same institution publicly recognizes cross-boundary AI/system interaction risk internally while mobilizing capital into the physical and digital substrate externally.

### Goldman Sachs — operating-model reconstruction + infrastructure finance

Observed internal environment:
- 2025 annual report describes One Goldman Sachs 3.0 as a new operating model propelled by AI.
- Goldman states AI benefit requires timely, accurate, complete data and a front-to-back view of people, decision-making, productivity, efficiency, and resilience—not merely retooling platforms.
- Earlier annual-report disclosures emphasize regular receipt of confidential client information and the institutional importance of preserving that confidence.

Observed capital role:
- Goldman research says hyperscalers could spend $5.3T on AI/data centers through 2030.
- Private infrastructure funds had more than $1.7T AUM and about $400B dry powder as of Sep 2025; Goldman expects private capital to be increasingly important for data-center buildout.

MR read:
Goldman is simultaneously restructuring its own operating model around data/AI and participating in/analysing the capital machinery underwriting external compute expansion. Internal governance artifact detail is less explicit than Equifax/JPMorgan; classify as partial control pending deeper evidence.

### BlackRock — infrastructure capital strong; internal coherence evidence incomplete

Observed:
- GIP flagship fund closed at $25.2B.
- BlackRock's AI Infrastructure Partnership is investing in new and expanded AI infrastructure and agreed to acquire Aligned Data Centers.
- 2025 letter argued a single AI data center may cost $40B–$50B and that private markets will be required to fund the scale of buildout.

MR read:
Very strong infrastructure-capital signal; insufficient evidence in this round to classify BlackRock's internal AI governance substrate at the same level as Equifax/JPMorgan. Keep as capital-side comparator, not full convergence control yet.

## Cross-case pattern

The strongest observed institutions are converging on:
1. consolidated/shared compute or cloud substrate;
2. protected proprietary/customer/non-public data;
3. centralized policy/governance with federated execution;
4. explicit human and machine identity/access control;
5. shared AI platforms rather than unmanaged tool proliferation;
6. production monitoring, model review, or policy-as-code;
7. enterprise risk integration;
8. large-scale capital commitments to the physical/digital infrastructure beneath AI.

This does not prove Measures Registry demand.

It does support the narrower proposition that capital-rich institutions are spending heavily to manufacture computational-system coherence around AI, and that the economic value they protect includes data, control, continuity, and operational resilience—not merely model performance.

## Emerging asymmetry hypothesis — held

Capital-rich institutions can internalize environmental coherence through large technology, cloud, security, risk, identity, and data investments. Less-capitalized institutions may face the same need without being able to manufacture a private substrate at comparable scale.

Research status: plausible; not yet quantified as market demand.

## Strategic-custody signal

Supported evidence:
- JPMorgan explicitly protects customer/company data inside LLM Suite.
- Equifax explicitly calls unique non-public data an AI data moat and competitive advantage.
- Goldman emphasizes preserving confidential client information.
- Bank of America describes extensive reliance on secure access management and protection of sensitive information across first- and third-party systems.

Interpretation:
The value of internal environmental coherence includes minimizing unnecessary disclosure of strategically sensitive data, topology, controls, and institutional decision conditions. Do not elevate this from interpretation to fact without direct evidence.

## Next excavation

For each convergence-control institution, trace:
- who owns compute/cloud substrate;
- who owns identity/permissions;
- who owns model/tool approval;
- where policy is actually enforced at runtime;
- what artifacts are portable across business-unit/vendor boundaries;
- what remains private/proprietary;
- what capital expenditure or operating cost built the substrate;
- whether governance spend can be isolated from general modernization;
- what smaller institutions use instead when they cannot build comparable substrate.
