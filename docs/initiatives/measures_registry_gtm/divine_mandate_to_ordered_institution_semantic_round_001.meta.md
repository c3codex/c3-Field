## Research question
Can ancient Mesopotamian evidence support a semantic chain of:

divine mandate → ruler/agent → plan/design → measurement/survey → materials/procurement → construction → ordered institution

without flattening art, ritual, theology, and governance into a modern technical model?

## Primary evidence seam: Gudea / Eninnu temple-building corpus

### 1. Divine mandate
Gudea Cylinder A begins with a divine decision concerning the building of Ningirsu's temple. Enlil approves; Ningirsu communicates the temple-building mandate to Gudea in a dream.

### 2. Ruler / acting agent
Gudea is the human ruler charged with carrying the work into effect. The text repeatedly places him as the temple-builder who must interpret, prepare, procure, measure, build, and dedicate.

### 3. Plan / design
In the dream interpretation sequence, Nisaba appears with a stylus and stellar tablet, while Nindub holds a lapis-lazuli tablet and sets down the plan/design of the house. ORACC renders Nindub as making the temple's plan conform to the instructions.

### 4. Measurement / survey
The Gudea 'Architect with Plan' statue preserves an architectural plan on a tablet together with a graduated measuring tool/surveyor's rod. Scholarship on the Ningirsu temple corpus identifies the rod and peg as survey tools used to establish building ground plans. Cylinder A also describes Gudea stretching lines, marking squares, aligning bricks with a string, and laying foundations.

### 5. Materials / procurement
Cylinder A explicitly traces cedar, other woods, copper/metals, bitumen, stone, and other resources from distant regions toward Lagash/Girsu. The Louvre summarizes materials converging from the Mediterranean-to-Indus world into the project.

### 6. Construction
The text proceeds through purification, foundation work, brick laying, walls, structural placement, and temple completion. Construction is not detached from the prior mandate, plan, measurement, or procurement stages.

### 7. Ordered institution
The completed temple is not merely a building. It becomes a durable cultic, administrative, ritual, and political center whose standing is tied to divine authorization, royal execution, measured form, material realization, dedication, and maintenance. Scholarship on the corpus explicitly reads its imagery in terms of authority, righteousness, order/chaos, kingship, and the divine temple.

## Inanna / Ishtar adjacency
The British Museum preserves an Old Babylonian seal representing Ishtar holding the measuring-rod and ring of divinity. This supports placement of Ishtar inside the wider Mesopotamian symbolic field of divine authority + measure, but does not by itself establish the full Gudea construction sequence as specifically Inanna's sequence.

## Strongest historically defensible semantic chain

divine instruction / authorization
→ appointed human ruler
→ interpreted design
→ measured/surveyed form
→ mobilized materials and labor
→ constructed and consecrated structure
→ enduring institutional order

## Important qualification
This is a semantic correspondence, not a claim that ancient Mesopotamians possessed a modern computational-governance framework. Art, ritual, theology, kingship, economics, construction, and institutional order are intertwined in the sources. The value of the comparison lies in the repeated structural relation among authorization, plan, measure, material, execution, and durable order.

## Research implication for current GTM work
The present computational-systems research may test whether a consequential system remains ordered by tracing a comparable structural sequence:

authority → plan/purpose → measurement → resources → construction/deployment → operation → institutional consequence

This is research framing only. It does not canonicalize historical symbolism as Measures Registry authority.
