# Experiment 001 — Healthcare Matched-Pair Falsification Round 001

status: research_only
branch: gtm-ledger-v1
operator: op044

## Question
Can public evidence falsify the hypothesis that stronger relational-governance coverage predicts lower reconstruction burden / cleaner exception and return handling in otherwise comparable ambient-AI clinical deployments?

## Result
INCONCLUSIVE; experiment design partially broken for public-data execution.

## Crack 1 — independent variable not observable enough
Large-scale ambient-scribe deployments in Spain show strong operational outcomes at million-encounter scale, including high adoption, stable semantic agreement / transcription accuracy, improved clinician experience, and shorter consultation times. However, public papers do not expose enough governance-state detail to classify those environments as lower-governance than Cleveland Clinic or other US academic centers.

Absence of public governance detail cannot be treated as weak governance.

## Crack 2 — dependent variable not reported
The public ambient-AI literature predominantly reports:
- adoption
- documentation time
- clinician burnout / cognitive load
- patient experience
- note quality / accuracy
- support burden

It generally does not report the variables Experiment 001 was designed to compare:
- reconstruction hours
- unresolved exception count
- return-state completeness
- final standing after change
- time to reconstruct authority provenance
- number of consequential relationships with current governance state

Therefore the core dependent variable is usually unavailable.

## Counterevidence against a simple governance-strength story
1. Spain multicenter outpatient deployment: >2.33M assisted encounters; adoption rose to ~31%; semantic agreement stable ~87.4–89.2%; clinician-experience improvements.
2. Spain emergency-care deployment: >1.03M scribe-assisted consultations across 48 EDs; ~21.8% shorter consultations; ~93.9% transcription accuracy; higher documentation quality; positive clinician/patient experience.
3. Multicenter US Abridge QI study: burnout declined from 51.9% to 38.8% over 30 days across six health systems, despite heterogeneous organizational environments.
4. Randomized trial of DAX vs Nabla: both improved several clinician outcomes; Nabla reduced time-in-note while DAX did not; clinically significant inaccuracies were reported only occasionally and similarly across vendors. This shows model/vendor differences can affect some operational outcomes independent of any measured governance variable.

## What this breaks
The original Experiment 001 protocol assumed public evidence could support a matched comparison of governance coverage vs reconstruction burden. It generally cannot.

So the public-data version of Experiment 001 cannot establish the proposed relationship and should not be treated as a valid causal test.

## What it does NOT break
It does not falsify the broader relational-governance thesis, because the necessary governance and reconstruction variables were not observed.

## Revised experimental requirement
A valid test needs institution-level access to at least:
- current consequential-relationship inventory
- authority / delegation state by relationship
- change-control history
- exception / incident records
- evidence-retention / return-state records
- reconstruction effort / time / cost

Without those, public comparisons can only test operational AI outcomes, not the Relational Governance Gap.

## Stronger falsifier for next round
Find a health system with demonstrably low governance coverage (not merely low public disclosure) that nevertheless shows:
- low reconstruction burden
- complete exception closure
- clean authority lineage
- durable return/disposition
- stable outcomes over change

If that exists repeatedly, the thesis weakens materially.
