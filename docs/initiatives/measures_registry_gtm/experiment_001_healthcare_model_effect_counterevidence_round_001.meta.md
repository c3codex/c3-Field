# Purpose

Attempt to falsify or materially weaken the working hypothesis that governance failure or burden is driven more strongly by relational governance gaps than by model capability alone.

# Counterevidence found

A randomized clinical trial in a single large academic health system assigned 238 outpatient physicians across 14 specialties to Microsoft DAX Copilot, Nabla, or usual care. Because the organizational environment was substantially shared while the AI product differed, this is a useful partial control on the governance environment.

Observed outcomes:
- Nabla users experienced a 9.5% decrease in time-in-note versus control (statistically significant).
- DAX users showed no significant time-in-note reduction versus control.
- Both scribe groups showed potential improvements in burnout/task-load measures.
- Clinically significant inaccuracies were reported occasionally for both products.

# Interpretation

This is real counterevidence against any broad claim that governance environment alone, or even primarily, determines operational outcome.

At least for documentation-efficiency outcomes, model/vendor capability can materially change results while the institutional environment remains similar.

Therefore the original working hypothesis must be narrowed.

Rejected broad form:

> Governance failure or burden is driven more strongly by relational governance gaps than by model capability alone.

Narrower testable form:

> For outcomes specifically involving authority continuity, evidence completeness, exception closure, change-state integrity, reconstruction burden, and accountable return/disposition across consequential relationships, relational governance gaps may explain variance not captured by model capability alone.

This does not claim relational governance gaps dominate all operational outcomes.

# Experiment consequence

Experiment 001 should not use productivity, documentation time, burnout, satisfaction, or simple adoption success as primary dependent variables for testing the relational-governance thesis. Those outcomes are materially influenced by product/model capability and usage intensity.

The experiment should instead seek outcomes closer to the proposed causal mechanism:
- authority reconstruction time,
- unresolved exception rate,
- evidence completeness after change,
- post-change review latency,
- disposition completeness,
- incident reconstruction burden,
- vendor-chain trace completeness,
- standing continuity across domain changes.

# Standing

- broad thesis: weakened
- narrow relational-governance thesis: not falsified
- Experiment 001 public-data design: still methodologically insufficient for causal proof
- model/vendor capability: confirmed material confounder and independent driver of at least some outcomes
- next falsification target: cases with similar model capability but directly observed differences in authority/evidence/return continuity

# Evidence basis

- Randomized clinical trial of two ambient AI scribes in a single academic health system, 238 physicians, 14 specialties, DAX vs Nabla vs control, 2024-11-04 through 2025-01-03.
- Result: Nabla significant 9.5% reduction in time-in-note vs control; DAX no significant change; both groups showed potential improvements in some clinician burden measures.

# Hold

Do not generalize this trial to all consequential outcomes or infer that governance has no effect. The trial primarily measured clinician productivity/burden and self-reported safety/accuracy, not cross-domain governance-memory outcomes.
