# Experiment 002 — Reconstruction Burden

status: research_active
branch: gtm-ledger-v1
operator: op044
scope: GTM research only; no Registry mutation

## Question

Does improving the governance machinery around consequential AI use materially reduce reconstruction burden, review-to-closure delay, or recurring governance bottlenecks while the underlying institutional work continues?

## Narrow thesis under test

For outcomes involving authority continuity, evidence completeness, exception closure, change-state integrity, reconstruction burden, and accountable return/disposition, relational governance conditions may explain burdens that model capability alone does not.

## Primary falsifier

If institutions with visibly fragmented or manual governance consistently show reconstruction burden comparable to institutions with coordinated, documented governance workflows — or if redesigning governance machinery does not reduce review/closure burden — this thesis weakens.

## Evidence round 001

### Case A — Large insurer; longitudinal governance redesign

Source: Grant Thornton, 2026, "Insurer streamlines AI governance to manage risk and spur innovation."

Observed pre-intervention conditions:
- backlog of more than 100 AI use-case review requests;
- manual use-case review process;
- free-form intake questionnaires;
- difficulty standardizing reviews, prioritizing use cases, and maintaining consistent risk evaluations;
- operational inefficiency and slowed decision-making.

Intervention:
- redesigned AI risk-review target state;
- redesigned intake questionnaire aligned to risk framework and organizational structure;
- preparation of use cases for review;
- cross-departmental risk discussions;
- documented governance rationale;
- workflow and business-requirement testing for a redesigned operating model.

Observed post-intervention outcome:
- more than 100 use cases reviewed while new requests continued entering the pipeline;
- shorter average use-case review-to-closure timelines;
- reduced governance bottlenecks;
- more sustainable steady state;
- simplified review process.

Evidence limitation:
- source does not publish the magnitude of review-to-closure reduction;
- source is a consultant-authored case study, not an independent controlled evaluation;
- intervention bundles process redesign, documentation, coordination, and workflow tooling, so the causal contribution of any one relational-governance element cannot be isolated.

Interpretation:
This is direct directional evidence that reconstruction/review burden moved when governance machinery changed, but not a sufficient effect-size estimate and not clean causal proof of the relational-governance thesis.

### Case B — Memorial Sloan Kettering; review-time reduction

Source surfaced in public web research: ALIGNMT AI case study claiming a 37% reduction in AI review time and centralized AI portfolio visibility.

Evidence classification:

- useful as a candidate replication case;
- not yet independently verified;
- not sufficient by itself for thesis confirmation.

### Case C — Rivian; privacy/AI review turnaround

Source surfaced in public web research: Clearly AI case study claiming 90% reduction in review turnaround time and 4x increase in review capacity.

Evidence classification:
- vendor-published;
- concerns privacy + AI governance review, not a pure consequential-act reconstruction test;
- useful as a candidate replication case;
- not sufficient by itself for thesis confirmation.

## Provisional result

Pitch 2 makes contact.

The insurer case supports the direction of the narrow thesis: fragmented/manual governance coincided with substantial review backlog and inconsistency; redesign toward structured intake, documented rationale, coordinated review, and workflow alignment coincided with shorter review-to-closure timelines and reduced bottlenecks.

But this round does NOT establish that relational governance state is the unique cause of the reduction. Process standardization, additional consulting labor, automation, and workflow technology are confounders.

## Standing

- reconstruction-burden hypothesis: supported directionally
- quantitative effect size: not established
- causal attribution to relational governance: unresolved
- independent replication: required
- thesis falsified: no
- home-run evidence: no

## Next swing

Find a case with either:
1. pre/post measurements of review or reconstruction burden where the governance-state intervention is separately identifiable; or
2. matched institutions performing similar work with similar AI capability but materially different authority/evidence/change/return coverage and reported closure burden.

## Evidence discipline

Do not convert vendor/consultant claims into independent findings. Preserve them as source-bounded evidence. Do not treat faster governance workflow as proof of complete authority continuity, return, or disposition unless those states are observed.
