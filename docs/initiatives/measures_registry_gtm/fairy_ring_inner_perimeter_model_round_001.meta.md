# Fairy Ring Inner-Perimeter Model — Round 001

## Purpose
Use the observed geometry of fungal fairy rings as a bounded research analogy for consequential computational environments. This is not a canonical c3 model and does not alter Source or Registry terminology.

## Scientific anchor
Contemporary fungal ecology describes fairy rings more accurately as expanding fungal fronts. Mycelium grows radially through soil; visible fruiting bodies commonly appear near the advancing outer edge; soil behind the front has already been altered by fungal occupation, decomposition, nutrient mobilization, hydrophobic effects, and senescent mycelium.

The relevant geometry is therefore not simply hidden network versus visible mushroom. It is a three-zone system:

1. **Unentered / prospective environment** — substrate and possible dependencies exist, but the active front has not yet made them consequential.
2. **Active consequential frontier** — the advancing edge where previously latent relationships become operative and visible enough to affect state, evidence, authority, or consequence.
3. **Lived interior / historical environment** — territory already traversed by the system, containing altered conditions, retained effects, prior states, and the consequences of past passage.

## Governance research translation
The analogy suggests a bounded alternative to exhaustive dependency mapping.

A governed environment need not know every dependency in the wider substrate. It must detect when a dependency reaches the consequential frontier and then preserve enough state to understand what crossed the boundary, under whose authority, under what conditions, with what evidence, and with what effect.

### Zone A — Prospective substrate
Possible vendors, model providers, cloud services, subcontractors, data sources, identity providers, infrastructure dependencies, and policy dependencies may exist without yet exercising consequential influence on the governed act.

Research question:
- What conditions cause a latent dependency to become consequential?

### Zone B — Fruiting frontier / active perimeter
A dependency enters the active perimeter when it can materially alter one or more of:
- evidence;
- authority;
- standing;
- decision state;
- execution;
- access;
- return;
- disposition;
- consequence.

Candidate rule:
**Circle the relationship when consequence fruits.**

This does not mean every technical call becomes a governed relationship. The threshold is material effect on the consequential act.

### Zone C — Lived interior
Once a relationship has crossed the frontier, its previous state cannot simply disappear when the frontier moves. The environment needs enough retained history to distinguish:
- what was true then;
- what authority existed then;
- what evidence was current then;
- what changed;
- what consequence occurred;
- why present standing differs now.

This is where the existing research concept of governance memory and the historical Inanna thesis of immutable/lived memory become structurally relevant, without treating the historical thesis as operative authority.

## Candidate measurement implications
For each consequential act, distinguish:

### Frontier detection rate
Number of newly consequential relationships detected when they first cross the active perimeter / all newly consequential relationships later discovered.

### Perimeter coverage
Consequential relationships inside the active perimeter with current, verifiable sufficient governance state / all consequential relationships inside the active perimeter.

### Historical continuity coverage
Previously consequential relationships whose authority/evidence/standing/change history remains reconstructable / all previously consequential relationships requiring later reconstruction.

### Latent-to-consequential transition time
Time between a dependency first becoming capable of affecting the governed act and the environment recognizing it as consequential.

### Perimeter drift
Material relationships that became consequential after the last environment review but were not incorporated into current governance state.

These are candidate research measures only.

## Why the inner perimeter matters
The goal is not to map infinity. The goal is to establish the smallest sufficient perimeter that contains every relationship capable of materially changing the consequential act.

This creates an empirical test:

> An environment is sufficiently mapped when additional dependency discovery outside the perimeter no longer changes the identified authority, evidence, standing, execution, return, or consequence of the governed act.

That is a stronger stopping condition than 'all vendors identified.'

## Falsifiers
- If consequential effects routinely originate from dependencies that cannot be detected until after harm, frontier detection may be too weak to govern prospectively.
- If nearly every low-level technical dependency can materially affect the act, the perimeter may expand until the model becomes impractical.
- If ordinary vendor-risk, IAM, observability, and audit tooling already provides complete frontier detection and historical continuity across independent authority domains, this model adds little.
- If retained relational history creates privacy, deletion, or autonomy conflicts that cannot be bounded, the memory requirement may be overbroad.

## Research standing
The fairy-ring geometry is a research analogy supported by fungal-front ecology, not a canonical c3 construct. The useful structural proposition is:

**latent substrate → consequential frontier → lived interior**

And the candidate governance rule is:

**Follow the fruiting event back only far enough to identify the relationships that crossed into consequence; draw the inner perimeter there; preserve the lived state behind the moving frontier.**
