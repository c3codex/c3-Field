## Purpose
Test the Measures Registry thesis using operational denominators rather than program descriptions. Focus on whether declared governance corresponds to current, verifiable, live operational coverage.

## High-value denominator findings

### AAA / large US-Canada organizations
Source: American Arbitration Association 2026 benchmark survey, 500 senior legal/executive leaders; 70% of represented organizations reported revenue of $1B+.

- 87% reported some form of AI governance in place.
- 22% reported those governance systems operating effectively.
- 33% reported defined escalation pathways when AI systems misbehave.
- 22% were very confident they could produce evidence of governance decisions for regulators/auditors.
- 35% reported legal/compliance involvement, versus 80% IT/technology involvement.

Interpretation: strong evidence of a gap between declared governance and operational governance. This is an artifact-continuity / evidence / response problem, not proof of absence of governance.

### KPMG 2026 AI pulse
- 66% reported monitoring dashboards.
- 61% reported approval processes.
- 26% reported full real-time cost visibility into AI at scale.

Interpretation: visible governance surfaces can coexist with incomplete runtime/economic observability.

### ISACA 2026 pulse
- 43% completely/fairly confident they could investigate and explain a serious AI incident to leadership/regulators.
- 39% completely/fairly confident in AI-related data governance.
- 36% said humans approve most AI-generated actions before execution.
- 20% did not know how humans oversee AI decision-making in their organization.

Interpretation: human-oversight claims need denominator testing against actual execution coverage and evidence retention.

### CSA 2026 / commissioned vendor study — directional only
Source caveat: commissioned by AI/identity security vendors; use as directional evidence unless independently corroborated.

- 82% reported previously unknown AI agents in their environments.
- 65% reported agent-related incidents in prior 12 months.
- 21% reported formal decommissioning processes.
- 68% claimed strong visibility despite 82% finding unknown agents.

Interpretation: perception of coverage can diverge sharply from discovered runtime state. Particularly relevant to identity, inventory, lifecycle and retirement artifacts.

### Grant Thornton 2026 — consulting-interest source
- Nearly 3 in 4 organizations reported giving agentic AI access to data/processes.
- 20% reported a tested AI incident-response plan.

Interpretation: access can scale before response artifacts mature. Treat source as corroborative, not controlling.

### Federal audit evidence
GAO / IRS 2026:
- IRS reported 126 active AI use cases as of June 2025.
- GAO found the AI inventory incomplete and recommended comprehensive quality-assurance and guidance for inventory entries.

GAO federal requirements review:
- Of 23 civilian CFO Act agencies reviewed, 10 implemented all selected AI requirements, 12 implemented some but not all, and 1 was exempt.
- GAO made 35 recommendations to 19 agencies.
- 15 agencies were told to update AI use-case inventories to include required information.
- As of March 2026, HHS still lacked all required information in its 2025 AI inventory.

USDA OIG 2026:
- Found cybersecurity and governance controls not fully implemented in AI systems.
- Recommended continual AI-inventory review/update and system-impact/risk/authorization assessment before AI technologies are allowed on the network.

Interpretation: inventories are required artifacts yet still become incomplete, stale, or quality-deficient even in regulated public institutions.

## China control / contrast
China is included as a regulatory-control comparator, not as evidence of superior governance.

Official CAC filing counts:
- 2024-12-31: 302 generative-AI services filed; 105 AI applications/functions registered.
- 2025-12-31: 748 services filed; 435 applications/functions registered.
- 2026-02-28: 796 services filed; 481 applications/functions registered.
- 2026-04-30: 868 services filed; 530 applications/functions registered.

Operational artifact requirement:
- Deployed generative-AI applications/functions within scope must publicly display the filed/registered service used, including model name and filing/registration number.

Additional scale signal:
- CAC reported 602 million generative-AI users by Dec 2025, 42.8% penetration.

Interpretation:
China provides a useful counterexample where model/service identity and regulatory registration are explicitly externalized as required artifacts at scale. This does NOT establish complete runtime governance, cross-boundary authority continuity, evidence portability, exception handling, or institutional autonomy. It does show that artifact existence and public model-service linkage can be mandated and scaled.

## Current research proposition
The highest-value denominator is not "does governance exist?" but:

> What percentage of the live consequential environment is covered by current, verifiable artifacts that remain bound to the system as identity, permissions, model versions, vendors, tools, workflows, and institutional relationships change?

Operational denominator classes:
1. live inventory coverage / discovered inventory
2. current approval coverage / deployed systems
3. re-review events / material change events
4. monitored runtime / consequential runtime
5. actions with retained evidence / consequential actions
6. incidents with tested escalation / incidents detected
7. identities with lifecycle custody / active computational identities
8. retired identities actually decommissioned / identities no longer needed
9. decisions with reconstructable authority / consequential decisions
10. cross-boundary relationships with portable governance artifacts / total consequential cross-boundary relationships

## Falsification condition
Measures Registry differentiation weakens if broad independent evidence shows that most institutions already maintain complete, current, portable, verifiable, low-cost governance artifacts across system/vendor/institution boundaries, including runtime correspondence, lifecycle closure, exception handling, evidence retention, and disposition.

## Current read
Evidence currently supports a narrower statement:

> Declared governance is substantially more common than demonstrated operational coverage. The recurring weakness appears at the boundary between governance artifacts and the live computational environment: inventory quality, change/re-review, identity lifecycle, runtime observability, escalation, evidence production, and cross-boundary continuity.

No conclusion is drawn here regarding deliberate market misdirection, suppression, or coordinated intent.
