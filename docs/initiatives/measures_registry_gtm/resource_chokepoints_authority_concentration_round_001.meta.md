# Question
Does rising AI/data-center compute demand simultaneously increase physical resource dependence, grid dependence, platform/model lock-in, and jurisdictional burden?

# Findings

## Critical mineral dependence
- IEA reports China accounts for around 99% of global refined gallium supply; data-center gallium demand by 2030 could exceed 10% of today's global supply.
- USGS reports U.S. net import reliance at 100% for gallium, arsenic, tantalum, fluorspar, indium; rare-earth compounds/metals at 80%.
- USGS identifies copper, silver, gold, tin, tantalum, palladium, platinum, arsenic, gallium, germanium, indium, rare earths and other minerals as relevant to data-center/server/chip infrastructure.
- S&P Global projects data-center copper demand rising from ~1.1 Mt in 2025 to ~2.5 Mt in 2040, with AI-training data centers accounting for 58% of data-center copper demand by 2030; non-crypto facilities roughly 30–40 t Cu/MW installed, with AI-training examples ~44–47 t/MW.

## Grid dependence and jurisdiction
- PJM reports major load growth from data centers and other large loads; 2026 plans include a large-load/data-center registry and emergency curtailment concepts requiring state/utility cooperation.
- Reuters reported PJM transmission-constraint costs reached ~$6B in H1 2026, with Northern Virginia and other data-center-heavy regions among the most affected.
- New York enacted a one-year moratorium on new data centers >=50 MW in July 2026 amid electricity, water, land-use and community concerns.
- Reuters reported 142 data-center protests across 42 U.S. states in July 2026, showing local political/jurisdictional burden is no longer marginal.
- Reuters reported xAI power infrastructure in Tennessee/Mississippi under dispute over permitting and local pollution burden, a concrete case of compute demand crossing corporate, state, federal, environmental and community authority domains.

## Platform/model concentration
- FTC 6(b) report documents Microsoft-OpenAI, Amazon-Anthropic, and Google-Anthropic partnerships with equity/revenue-sharing, cloud-spend commitments, consultation/control/exclusivity rights, discounted compute, sensitive technical/business information sharing, and product/platform integration opportunities.
- FTC notes potential switching costs and scarcity effects in compute and engineering talent; these are competition concerns, not proof of unlawful control.

# Structural read
The same expansion of computation produces multiple dependency concentrations at once:
1. mineral/refining concentration;
2. grid and interconnection concentration;
3. cloud/model/platform concentration;
4. local jurisdictional burden concentration.

This does not prove coordinated intent or sovereign status. It does support the proposition that computational expansion is accompanied by increased dependency depth and more authority boundaries.

# Research proposition
A consequential computational environment should be measured not only by model/workload characteristics but by the resource, infrastructure, platform, and jurisdictional dependencies necessary for the act to occur.

# Candidate measurements
- Critical Resource Dependency Count
- Import/Processing Concentration Exposure
- Grid Dependency Intensity
- Platform Switching Burden
- Jurisdictional Crossing Count
- Consequence-to-Value Separation
- Governed Relationship Coverage across physical + computational layers

# Holds
- No inference that all AI demand directly causes all grid/mineral stress.
- No inference that concentration equals conspiracy or deliberate memory erasure.
- No claim that MR currently solves these dependencies.
- No Registry mutation or Source canonicalization.
