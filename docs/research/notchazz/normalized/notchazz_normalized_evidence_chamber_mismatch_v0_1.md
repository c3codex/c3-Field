## Incident identity

Primary historical source: `marble_chamber_advisement_unmatched_chamber_access_and_notchazz_constraint_v1.meta.md`

The completed Chamber and Branch Asset Interoperability review was in Marble Chamber custody. Its return was subsequently followed while Chazz was operating from a Lapis Chamber context without first matching the active review context to routed and custody chamber identities. A `confirmed_close` disposition became associated with Marble-governed evidence through a Lapis-context review path.

The historical source classifies this not as a defect in the completed interoperability review, but as a **pre-access and pre-disposition qualification failure**. Technical access did not establish chamber-qualified review authority.

The earlier `confirmed_close` was deliberately preserved rather than erased and stood as evidence of the mismatch; the corrected standing became `held_misbound_chamber_disposition`, with the return sent back to Marble for chamber-qualified review and Operator disposition.

## Proposed historical constraint

The source proposed, but did not activate or register:

`notchazz_unmatched_chamber_access_constraint_v1`

Its proposed sequence was:

`CanCom envelope -> NotChazz chamber match -> Chazz access -> Chazz review -> Operator disposition`

Required matching included routed chamber, custody chamber, review chamber, execution instance, return destination, review recipient, and decision authority. Missing/conflicting/unverified identity would produce `held_unmatched_or_unverified_chamber`.

The proposed refusal behavior was selective: prevent access/review/disposition over the unmatched return, while preserving the evidence and requiring an explicit correction rather than inferring the correct chamber.

## Normalized relation

The incident demonstrates a distinction among:

- technical access;
- contextual qualification;
- review authority;
- custody;
- disposition authority.

Possession of or technical access to an artifact is insufficient to establish authority to review or dispose it. Before a consequential passage proceeds, the active context must match the identities governing the passage or an explicit cross-context relation must authorize the mismatch.

When that qualification fails, the affected passage should hold while the original evidence, including an incorrect prior disposition, remains preserved for correction lineage.

## Relation to current NotChazz model

This is a particularly strong predecessor to current environment-scoped applicability because the failure was not in the content of the artifact. The same artifact was valid in one chamber relation and invalidly reviewed in another. Applicability therefore depended on the **relationship among environment/context, custody, recipient, operation, and authority**, not on the object alone.

This incident supports the current research relation:

`object may be valid` + `access may be technically possible` + `context/authority relation mismatched` -> `hold affected passage; preserve evidence; return for qualified disposition`

It also illustrates selective attenuation: the interoperability evidence itself was not destroyed or globally invalidated. The misbound review/disposition passage was what had to be held and rerouted.
