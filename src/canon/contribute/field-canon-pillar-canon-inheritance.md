# Field Canon and Pillar Canon Inheritance

## Purpose

Defines the relationship between c3 Field canon and pillar-specific canon.

Ensures canonical structure remains coherent as the system expands.

---

# Field Canon

c3 Field maintains **13 canonical records**.

These records function as the constitutional memory of the environment.

They define:

- system philosophy  
- governance boundaries  
- architectural principles  
- canonical meaning  

Field canon represents the highest canonical authority in the system.

---

# Pillar Canon

Each pillar may maintain a **pillar-specific canon** necessary for its internal coherence.

Examples of pillars:

- Measures  
- Priceless Gallery  
- c3 Model  
- CoherentAI  

Pillar canon exists to interpret and operationalize field canon within a specific domain.

---

# Canon Relationship

The relationship between canon layers is hierarchical.

Field Canon  
→ Pillar Canon  
→ Operational Systems

Operational systems include:

- registries  
- installations  
- exhibitions  
- applications  
- governance processes  

---

# Pillar Canon Constraints

Pillar canon may:

- interpret field canon  
- specialize field canon  
- define domain-specific logic  
- organize pillar-level artifacts  

Pillar canon may not:

- contradict field canon  
- detach from field canon  
- redefine constitutional principles  
- operate as an independent canon system  

---

# Architectural Implication

Field canon defines what must remain true.

Pillar canon defines what must remain coherent within a domain.

Registries and applications implement those rules operationally.

---

# Practical Structure

Example:

Field Canon (13 records)

↓

Measures Canon

↓

Measures Registry  
Measures Installation  
Measures Exhibition

Each layer inherits coherence from the layer above.

---

# Closing Principle

Pillars are distinct chambers within the c3 Field architecture.

They maintain their own canonical structure while remaining fully inherited from field canon.

No pillar canon exists independently of the field.
