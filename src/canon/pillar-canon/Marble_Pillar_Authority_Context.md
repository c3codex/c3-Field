# On Authority and Transmission

## Marble Pillar Context Text

Within the ancient Mesopotamian imagination, the MEs were understood as
foundational measures — the structural principles that make
civilization possible. They encompassed craft, governance, ritual,
language, and social order.

Traditionally, these measures were held in the domain of Enki, the
keeper of wisdom, architecture, and subterranean knowledge.

In this exhibition, the Codexstone may be understood as that axial
reservoir — not as a deity represented, but as the structural source
from which measures originate.

Inanna's authority does not arise from possession alone. It arises from
her capacity to carry the MEs into relational space.

If the Codexstone represents contained architecture,  
Inanna represents enacted articulation.

The Marble MEs are not trophies removed from origin.  
They are measures brought into visibility.

Authority here is not ownership.  
It is transmission.

The Codexstone remains constant.  
The MEs become legible through Inanna's passage.

The exhibition does not stage conflict between source and bearer.  
It presents a structural relationship:

Wisdom held.  
Measure carried.  
Architecture revealed.
