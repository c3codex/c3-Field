# Obsidian — The Measure of Reduction

The Obsidian pillar articulates descent as a measured process.

In ancient Mesopotamian imagination, the crossing of thresholds was
structured, not chaotic. Each gate marked a deliberate relinquishment.
Ornament, title, protection, and position were not stripped arbitrarily;
they were measured against structural integrity.

The Seven Obsidian Gates present reduction as calibration.

Each passage removes an external marker of authority in order to expose
internal structure. This reduction is not punishment and not spectacle.
It is assessment.

Obsidian absorbs reflection. It does not amplify surface. Within this
pillar, authority is tested through subtraction.

What remains after each gate is not diminished presence, but clarified
form.

The Measure of Reduction does not dramatize loss. It renders excess
visible and releases it.

Descent, in this context, is not collapse. It is alignment through
removal.
